# Evidence-first research — Standard / Deep

## Procedure
1. Frame the **job/customer/payer** (distinct if applicable), alternative solutions, geography, stage and adoption blockers. Collect user-provided data before web searches.
2. Search authoritative sources first: statistics offices, regulators, company filings, published research. Then official product/pricing pages, trade bodies and direct interview/survey evidence. Prefer recent, relevant statistics; record the *measurement* year separately from publication date.
3. Standard: study 3–5 direct/adjacent competitors and 2–4 relevant market/data sources per decisive claim. Deep: expand only disputed segments, markets or high-impact assumptions. No fabricated citation, no guessing a competitor's metrics from traffic alone.
4. For each major claim log `evidence_id`, claim, source URL or local document reference, issuer, observation date, verified_at date, population/sample/method, confidence (high|medium|low), applicability, limitations. Source reliability ≠ generalizability to this business.
5. Explicitly list **disconfirming evidence** and failed hypotheses: unavailable willingness to pay, unsubsidized substitute, price sensitivity, seasonal demand, channel dependence, data gaps.
6. User interview/survey percentages require **N**, methodology, sample frame and wording before being labeled representative. Stated willingness to pay is not observed conversion.

## Stop rules
- If no reliable public market size, model a bounded, sourced estimate and say why.
- If competitor sources are insufficient, mark unknown rather than invent ranking, revenue, features or pricing.
- If material uncertainty persists, ask only the decision-relevant question or produce a sensitivity range.
