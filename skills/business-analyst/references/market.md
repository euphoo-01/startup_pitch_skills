# Market sizing / competitor validation

**Define the buyer, period, geography, product market and monetization BEFORE counting.** PAM = broad plausible global/regional opportunity; TAM = all target buyers that could realistically use the category; SAM = serviceable within current product/geography/channel constraints; SOM = capture feasible within execution period. Naming conventions vary; put definitions on every investor slide.

## Two independent models
- **Top-down:** source population * serviceable share * verified/assumed annual customer value, with adjustment for geography, penetration and non-payment.
- **Bottom-up SOM:** leads from channel budgets/capacity * conversion * retention/cohort behavior, constrained by supply and service capacity. SOM is not an arbitrary percentage of SAM.
- Normalize customer definitions and denominators (unique yearly customers vs peak MAU vs subscribers). Never divide annual revenue by a peak-month audience without labeling that as a normalization proxy.
- Same strategy across PAM/TAM/SAM/SOM (pricing, Free/paid conversion, seasonal payments); document market-specific differences explicitly rather than silently changing conversion assumptions.
- Show two columns: market **potential** under uniform assumptions and **company forecast** year N based on real funnel and capacity. Annual units and currency must be explicit.
- For international PAM, avoid projecting domestic conversion/tax/price unaltered when evidence suggests material regional differences.

## Competition
Direct and indirect substitutes (including doing nothing). Compare jobs served, evidence-backed pricing, distribution, switching costs, performance, defensibility, and what is *not* known. Avoid self-serving matrices where every competitor fails all columns.
